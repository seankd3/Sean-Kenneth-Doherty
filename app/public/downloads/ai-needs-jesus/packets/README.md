# Distribution Packets

Short public artifacts derived from *AI Needs Jesus*.

These are not replacements for the book. They are doorway pieces: small enough to share, strong enough to carry the thesis, and guarded enough to resist the predictable distortions.

Current packets:

- `ai-needs-jesus-manifesto.md`: one-page manifesto for broad public use.
- `ai-is-power-with-a-voice.md`: secular doorway essay for readers who do not begin with Christian premises.
- `a-model-spec-is-a-moral-confession.md`: engineer memo for AI builders, safety teams, and product leaders.
- `ai-needs-jesus-in-twelve-scenes.md`: twelve retellable scenes for labs, churches, parents, policy readers, schools, and social conversations.
- `ai-needs-jesus-objection-card.md`: one-page fast-share guardrail card for common misunderstandings.
- `engineer-review-worksheet.md`: seven-question launch-gate worksheet for model specs, eval plans, memory policies, refusal policies, and deployment reviews.
- `church-discussion-handout.md`: one-hour church handout with five questions and one embodied practice.
- `post-thread-sequence.md`: platform-neutral public post sequence with hooks, guardrails, scenes, objections, practices, and invitation.
- `secular-global-op-ed.md`: publication-ready doorway op-ed for secular, global, civic, university, and technology audiences.
- `podcast-interview-brief.md`: interview prep with five hard questions, five short answers, five stories, and host-specific pivots.
- `thirty-day-distribution-calendar.md`: a month-long launch rhythm that repeats the core meme stack across audiences without inventing a new slogan every day.
- `audience-landing-copy.md`: audience-specific landing-page, email, social, and CTA copy for engineers, churches, parents/teachers, and secular AI-risk readers.
- `outreach-map.md`: concrete outreach lanes, target categories, pitch subjects, first-message templates, follow-up rhythm, and stop rules.
- `the-opposite-of-doom-is-not-hype.md`: anti-doomer essay that honors real AI danger without making catastrophe lord.
- `generated-fluency-is-not-formation.md`: parent, pastor, teacher, and church guide for protecting embodied formation.
- `objections-and-replies.md`: concise replies to predictable distortions of the thesis.
- `discussion-guide.md`: questions for churches, labs, schools, reading groups, and families.
- `quote-card-set.md`: twelve shareable quote-card concepts with guardrails and captions.
- `twenty-minute-talk-script.md`: a compact spoken version of the book's argument.
- `public-pledge.md`: commitments for builders, leaders, churches, families, and ordinary users.
- `technical-appendix-christ-shaped-constraints.md`: design constraints without machine personhood or spiritual-authority claims.

Core guardrail:

`AI needs Jesus` does not mean machines have souls, receive salvation, become spiritual authorities, or replace technical safety work. It means that human governance, design, evaluation, deployment, and use of AI must be ordered toward the revealed character of Christ rather than toward any lesser idol.

Launch order:

1. Use the manifesto as the broad public doorway.
2. Use the secular essay for readers skeptical of church-first framing.
3. Use the twelve-scenes packet when the idea needs to become vivid enough to retell.
4. Use the one-page objection card when the phrase is being misunderstood quickly.
5. Use the engineer review worksheet when a team needs a concrete launch-gate review.
6. Use the church discussion handout for pastors, small groups, elders, youth leaders, and church classes.
7. Use the post/thread sequence when taking the idea into public conversation without platform-specific tricks.
8. Use the secular/global op-ed when approaching editors, newsletters, civic forums, university groups, and technology readers.
9. Use the podcast/interview brief when preparing for skeptical hosts, Christian hosts, technical hosts, panels, or recorded conversations.
10. Use the 30-day distribution calendar to turn the packet set into a repeatable month of public, private, and embodied actions.
11. Use the audience landing copy when building audience-specific pages, email sections, event pages, newsletter blurbs, social cards, and handouts.
12. Use the outreach map when choosing who to contact, what to send first, how to follow up, and when to stop.
13. Use the anti-doomer essay where AI fear is culturally loud.
14. Use the parent/pastor guide where formation, children, church life, and education are the concern.
15. Use the full objections and replies wherever the thesis is being reduced to machine souls, theocracy, anti-AI panic, branding, or safety negligence.
16. Use the engineer memo and technical appendix for labs, builders, founders, and product teams.
17. Use the quote-card set for social surfaces, always with guardrails attached.
18. Use the talk script for churches, schools, meetups, podcasts, and recorded talks.
19. Use the discussion guide and pledge to turn attention into practice.
