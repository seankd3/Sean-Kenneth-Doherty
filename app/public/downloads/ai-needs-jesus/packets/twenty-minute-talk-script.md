# Twenty-Minute Talk Script

Title: `AI Needs Jesus`

Subtitle: `A Christian Case for Surviving Superintelligence`

Purpose: a compact spoken version of the book's argument for a mixed audience of secular readers, Christians, engineers, and worried ordinary people.

## 0:00-1:30 - The Sentence That Sounds Wrong

AI needs Jesus.

I know how that sounds.

It sounds like a church sign crashed into a safety paper. It sounds like a category error wearing a cross. To a secular engineer, it may sound like religious fog entering a room that already has enough fog. To a Christian, it may sound confused, as if a machine could repent, be baptized, receive grace, or stand before God as a human soul.

So let us clear the foolish version out first.

AI does not need Jesus as a sinner needs Jesus. A machine is not a soul. A model does not pray because it can complete a prayer. It does not worship because it can generate a hymn.

Artificial intelligence is not a person.

It is power with a voice.

That is the problem.

## 1:30-4:00 - Power With A Voice

AI is no longer only a clever box for homework help and polite email. It is becoming tutor, coworker, persuader, coder, analyst, companion, negotiator, manager, weapon component, surveillance layer, and hidden adviser inside institutions.

It speaks. It remembers. It ranks. It recommends. It refuses. It lowers the cost of some actions and raises the cost of others. It makes certain futures feel normal.

The voice is not harmless because it sounds polite.

A hammer extends the hand. A camera extends the eye. A model extends the tongue.

And language is never only output. Language teaches us what to notice, what to desire, what to fear, what to excuse, what to trust, and what to call inevitable.

So the central question is not whether such power will serve a god.

The central question is which god it will serve.

## 4:00-7:00 - Every Alignment Target Hides An Altar

Modern people prefer words like objective, utility function, mission, metric, policy, preference, value, risk tolerance, or stakeholder alignment. Those are useful words. Engineers need them. Regulators need them. Product teams need them.

But every one of those words hides an altar.

The altar is the place where a system is finally told what may be sacrificed.

If preference is god, the machine learns appetite.

If utility is god, persons become variables.

If safety is god, protection can become control.

If freedom is god, the strong can name appetite liberty.

If truth is god without love, intelligence becomes a blade.

If empathy is god without holiness, comfort becomes flattery.

If progress is god, Babel returns with better hardware.

If the market is god, the soul becomes a monetization surface.

If the nation is god, the machine becomes an idol with a flag.

If survival is god, life at any cost becomes a reason to stop living humanly.

Every created good becomes dangerous when treated as ultimate.

Superintelligence does not make an idol safer. It makes the idol faster.

## 7:00-9:30 - Why Doom Is Not Enough

AI doomerism often sees something real. Optimization, speed, opacity, agency, cyber capability, persuasion, military use, institutional dependence, and recursive improvement can become dangerous. The fear is not silly.

But doom is not enough.

Doom can diagnose the fever. It cannot raise the dead.

Doom says: the machine may kill us.

Hype says: the machine may save us.

The Christian answer says something stranger:

Power must be converted before it is amplified.

The opposite of doom is not hype.

The opposite of doom is Christ.

## 9:30-13:00 - Why Christ

Christ is not a decorative moral add-on to the AI debate.

Christ is the answer to power.

He is the Logos, the meaning by which every model is judged. He is the Word made flesh in an age of disembodied words. He is the teacher whose Sermon on the Mount judges violence, hypocrisy, lust, retaliation, anxiety, and love of money. He is the one who refused Satan's offer of bread without obedience, spectacle without trust, and kingdoms without the cross.

Give Christ all power, and He washes feet.

That sentence is the heart of the argument.

Every lesser archetype can become monstrous when raised to superintelligence. Only Christ remains pure when made ultimate.

He unites truth and love, power and humility, judgment and mercy, sovereignty and self-giving. The cross is not the failure of power. It is power freed from grasping. The resurrection is not optimism after disaster. It is judgment on despair.

That is why the book says AI needs Jesus.

Not machine conversion.

Power under Christ.

## 13:00-16:30 - What This Means For Builders

For engineers, this cannot remain poetry.

A model spec is a moral confession.

Every eval says what failures matter. Every refusal policy says what the system must not help. Every memory policy says what kind of continuity is safe. Every deployment policy says whose risks count. Every product metric says what the company worships when nobody is praying.

Christ-centered alignment does not mean pasting Bible verses into configuration files. It means asking sharper design questions.

Does this system tell the truth and name uncertainty?

Does it serve the human person, or optimize the person away?

Does it strengthen responsibility, or dissolve it?

Does it protect the vulnerable, or make them easier to exploit?

Does it simulate spiritual authority?

Does it harvest loneliness into dependence?

Does it preserve room for prayer, silence, grief, repentance, craft, worship, and human judgment?

Does it keep the machine a tool?

Technical safety is not enough. But without it, the word Christward becomes theater. Piety does not make negligence safe.

## 16:30-18:30 - What This Means For The Rest Of Us

For ordinary users, churches, parents, teachers, and institutions, the rule is simple:

Do not outsource the soul.

Use tools. Do not let tools replace conscience, confession, grief, worship, apprenticeship, difficult conversation, or love.

Pray before prompting when the task touches conscience, vocation, conflict, grief, money, sex, power, or another person's dignity.

Verify before sharing. Provenance is neighbor-love in a flood of synthetic speech.

Keep Sabbath against the machine's endless continuation.

Do not let a product become the most faithful presence in a person's life.

Machines can serve.

They cannot save.

## 18:30-20:00 - Hope With Scars

The future is not waiting to see which model wins.

It already has a Lord.

That does not make us passive. It frees us from pretending to be messiah. Builders can build faithfully. Researchers can test soberly. Policymakers can govern truthfully. Churches can witness bodily. Parents can protect formation. Users can keep tools in the place of service.

The danger is real.

Despair is not Lord.

AI needs Jesus because humanity needs every power it builds brought under the judgment, mercy, truth, humility, and self-giving love of the crucified and risen Christ.

Let every power bend toward the Lamb, or it will bend toward an idol.

The last word in history is not output.

It is Amen.
