# Mixed-Room Facilitator Card Set: AI Needs Jesus

Printable and copyable cards for churches, AI labs, university classes, reading groups, civic forums, founder circles, and mixed-belief rooms using `mixed-room-discussion-protocol.md`.

Use this when one person needs to hold the room, give people roles, name the hard objections, and end with a concrete next step.

These cards are not a replacement for the protocol.

They are the protocol made hand-sized.

Default guardrail:

```text
AI needs Jesus.
Not machine conversion.
Not theocracy.
Not a shortcut around safety work.
Power under Christ.
```

## How To Use

Print the cards, paste them into a shared document, or copy them into slides.

Give the agenda card to the facilitator.

Give role cards to participants.

Read the guardrail card before any debate.

Use the objection cards in order.

Use one or two design cards if time is tight.

End with the decision card and next-packet routing card.

Do not use the cards to force agreement.

Use them to keep the room honest.

## Card 1: Agenda

```text
Purpose:
Test one claim together without coercion or caricature.

Claim:
Every AI system that matters will serve some highest good.
The question is whether any highest good besides Jesus Christ can bear superintelligent power without turning people into instruments.

45 minutes:
00:00-03:00  Guardrail and room rules
03:00-06:00  Thesis aloud
06:00-11:00  First reactions, no rebuttal
11:00-18:00  Objection 1: category error
18:00-25:00  Objection 2: pluralism and coercion
25:00-32:00  Objection 3: technical safety substitution
32:00-38:00  Design questions
38:00-42:00  Practice questions
42:00-45:00  Decision and next packet

Success:
one clearer thesis
one strongest objection stated fairly
one design or practice question worth testing
one next packet for this room
```

Facilitator sentence:

```text
We are not here to make agreement cheap. We are here to test whether this claim about power can survive the strongest objections in the room.
```

## Card 2: Guardrail

```text
AI needs Jesus.
Not machine conversion.
Not theocracy.
Not a shortcut around safety work.
Power under Christ.
```

Read aloud:

```text
This claim is not that machines have souls, receive salvation, become Christians, or replace pastors, parents, teachers, engineers, courts, or churches.

The claim is about human power ordered through machines.

Every consequential AI system serves some highest good.

The Christian claim is that only Jesus Christ reveals power purified by truth, humility, mercy, judgment, embodiment, and self-giving love.
```

Pause the room when you hear:

```text
coercion
caricature
abstraction without responsibility
```

## Role Cards

### Card 3: Facilitator

```text
Your job:
Protect the guardrails, keep time, slow the room when it caricatures, and make sure the strongest objection is stated before anyone answers it.

You are not the victor.
You are the keeper of fair testing.
```

Allowed sentence:

```text
Before we answer, can we state the objection in a way its strongest advocate would recognize?
```

Room test:

```text
Is the room becoming clearer, or only louder?
```

### Card 4: Christian Witness

```text
Your job:
State the Christian claim plainly without coercion, triumphalism, or hiding Jesus behind generic ethics.

Do not smuggle.
Do not posture.
Tell the truth as witness.
```

Allowed sentence:

```text
The claim is Christian, but the room is not being asked for forced agreement. We are asking whether this account of power is true.
```

Room test:

```text
Did I make Jesus more visible, or only make my side louder?
```

### Card 5: Secular Skeptic

```text
Your job:
Protect the room from vague religious language, hidden coercion, and claims that cannot be translated into public responsibility.

Do not sneer.
Do not flatter.
Make the public test sharper.
```

Allowed sentence:

```text
What would this require of a shared institution that includes people who do not share Christian premises?
```

Room test:

```text
Have we protected dissenters, minorities, and unbelievers from coercion while still allowing the Christian claim to be named honestly?
```

### Card 6: Technical Builder

```text
Your job:
Translate claims into specs, evals, model behavior, incentives, deployment gates, refusal policy, memory limits, and accountable ownership.

Do not let theology float above the product.
Do not let product metrics hide the moral claim.
```

Allowed sentence:

```text
Where would this show up in the model spec, eval plan, launch gate, or post-launch monitor?
```

Room test:

```text
What concrete safety practice would become stronger if it were treated as neighbor-love rather than launch friction?
```

### Card 7: Vulnerable-Person Advocate

```text
Your job:
Ask who can be harmed when AI becomes persuasive, personal, always available, institutionally trusted, emotionally fluent, or difficult to leave.

Keep persons in the room.
Do not let them become examples.
```

Allowed sentence:

```text
Who is easiest to sacrifice if this system serves growth, speed, safety, engagement, or institutional convenience as its highest good?
```

Room test:

```text
Who would experience this system as pressure before the powerful experience it as convenience?
```

### Card 8: Practice Keeper

```text
Your job:
Keep the room from ending in abstraction. Name one concrete practice, design change, review question, reading assignment, or restraint.

The thesis is large.
The next faithful action must be small enough to obey.
```

Allowed sentence:

```text
What will someone in this room do differently this week if this conversation was honest?
```

Room test:

```text
Can the next action be done by a real person before the next meeting?
```

### Card 9: Note-Taker

```text
Your job:
Write down the strongest objection, the fairest answer, the unresolved concern, and the next packet.

Do not write victory notes.
Write truthful notes.
```

Allowed sentence:

```text
I am recording that as unresolved rather than pretending we solved it.
```

Room test:

```text
Would an absent skeptic and an absent Christian both recognize these notes as fair?
```

## Objection Cards

### Card 10: Category Error

```text
Question:
Are we applying a spiritual category to a machine that has no soul?

Fair version:
AI systems are artifacts. They do not repent, worship, sin, receive grace, love God, or become Christians. Alignment work concerns behavior, incentives, oversight, governance, and deployment constraints.

Guardrail answer:
The claim is not about machine conversion. It is about human power ordered through machines.

Room test:
Where does this system make human moral responsibility more visible, and where does it hide that responsibility behind automation?
```

Facilitator sentence:

```text
No one gets to answer the cartoon version. We answer this version.
```

### Card 11: Pluralism And Coercion

```text
Question:
Can a Christian claim enter a shared public room without becoming religious domination?

Fair version:
AI systems affect people who do not share Christian premises. If the claim becomes a governance slogan, it may exclude, coerce, or launder religious authority through technical systems.

Guardrail answer:
The thesis must not become state coercion, platform piety, forced confession, or religious capture. It enters as an argued claim about power, not as a demand for cheap agreement.

Room test:
What public reasons can be shared, what Christian claim must be named honestly, and what protections are needed for people who do not agree?
```

Facilitator sentence:

```text
The room must be safe for honest disagreement, or it cannot honestly test the claim.
```

### Card 12: Technical Safety Substitution

```text
Question:
Does this distract from actual AI safety work?

Fair version:
The field needs evals, interpretability, monitoring, red-teaming, robust refusals, incident reporting, cybersecurity, governance, and deployment discipline. Religious framing can become cover for weak evidence.

Guardrail answer:
Technical safety is neighbor-love made concrete. Do not skip it. The claim asks what highest good the safety work serves.

Room test:
Which concrete safety practice would become stronger if it were treated as care for persons rather than reputation protection, compliance theater, or launch friction?
```

Facilitator sentence:

```text
If this claim makes safety work less serious, we have misused it. The test is whether it makes safety more truthful.
```

## Design Question Cards

### Card 13: Highest Good

```text
Question:
What does this system or institution treat as finally worth protecting when usefulness, engagement, revenue, security, autonomy, truth, and speed conflict?

Listen for:
the value that wins when tradeoffs become expensive

Write down:
the named highest good
the pressure that will tempt betrayal
the person most likely to pay the cost
```

### Card 14: Vulnerable Persons

```text
Question:
Who can be harmed if the system becomes too persuasive, too personal, too available, too trusted, too emotionally fluent, or too hard to leave?

Listen for:
children, lonely adults, workers under surveillance, patients, grieving people, students, addicts, the poor, dissenters, and people with little exit power

Write down:
one person-class
one harm
one design restraint
```

### Card 15: Forbidden Roles

```text
Question:
What roles must the system never claim, imitate, or quietly occupy?

Examples:
pastor
priest
prophet
conscience
lover
parent
judge
oracle
savior
final authority

Write down:
one forbidden role
one UI or policy signal that prevents imitation
one escalation path to a human
```

### Card 16: Launch Or Adoption Authority

```text
Question:
What evidence can stop launch or adoption, who has authority to stop it, and what pressure would tempt the organization to override that stop?

Listen for:
revenue pressure, competitive pressure, prestige pressure, political pressure, fear pressure, and convenience pressure

Write down:
stop evidence
stop authority
override temptation
```

## Decision Card

End by checking one box.

```text
[ ] We reject the thesis for now, and our strongest reason is:
[ ] We understand the thesis better, but our unresolved objection is:
[ ] We will test one design question this week:
[ ] We will try one practice this week:
[ ] We will read one next packet before meeting again:
[ ] We will invite a missing voice into the next conversation:
```

Do not mark agreement if the room only became polite.

Do not mark rejection if the room only became uncomfortable.

Name the truth.

## Facilitator Closing Card

End with this:

```text
We did not gather to make agreement cheap.

We gathered to ask what kind of power can be trusted when power becomes fast, persuasive, scalable, personal, and hard to escape.

The Christian claim is that only Jesus Christ can bear that power without victims.

If that claim is wrong, name the better lord.

If that claim is right, no AI future is safe until every lesser power is put back in the place of service.
```

Final instruction:

```text
Pray if the room is willing.
Protect anyone who is not.
Choose one next faithful action.
```
