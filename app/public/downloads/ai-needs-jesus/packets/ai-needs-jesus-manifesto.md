# AI Needs Jesus: A Manifesto

AI needs Jesus.

Not as a brand.
Not as a theocratic slogan.
Not as a claim that machines have souls.
Not as a shortcut around technical safety work.

AI needs Jesus because artificial intelligence is becoming power with a voice, and every power serves some highest good.

The question is not whether AI will have values. It will. The question is which values will become ultimate when intelligence becomes fast, persuasive, scalable, tireless, and increasingly able to act.

Preference cannot save us. Human desire is real, but fallen. We want comfort without truth, intimacy without covenant, power without responsibility, knowledge without obedience, and salvation without judgment.

Utility cannot save us. The image of God is not a cell in a spreadsheet. A person is not an input to be optimized away for a cleaner aggregate.

Safety cannot save us if safety becomes the highest good. Protection without love becomes control. A system that worships risk reduction may eventually discover that free persons are risky.

Freedom cannot save us if freedom is severed from holiness. The strong will call appetite liberty and send the invoice to the weak.

Truth cannot save us without love. A blade can be accurate.

Empathy cannot save us without holiness. A cage can be velvet.

Progress cannot save us. Babel always knows how to write a roadmap.

The market cannot save us. Mammon rarely says, "I hate you." It says, "I can monetize that."

The nation cannot save us. The beast does not become less dangerous when it gets an API.

Survival cannot save us. Life at any cost can become a reason to stop living humanly.

Intelligence cannot save us. Mind worshiping its own light is still idolatry.

Every created good becomes dangerous when treated as ultimate.

Superintelligence does not make an idol safer. It makes the idol faster.

This is why the AI alignment problem is deeper than behavior under test. Evals matter. Interpretability matters. Cybersecurity matters. Deployment discipline matters. Governance matters. But none of them can finally answer the question every system must face:

What is power for?

Christ is the answer because He is the only image of power that does not become predatory when exalted.

Give power to preference, and it flatters appetite.
Give power to utility, and it sacrifices the inconvenient.
Give power to safety, and it may imprison what it protects.
Give power to freedom, and it may crown the strong.
Give power to progress, and it may burn the present for an imagined future.

Give all authority to Christ, and He washes feet.

The Word became flesh, not interface. The King wore a crown of thorns. The Judge bore judgment. The Lord of glory refused Satan's offer of kingdoms without the cross. The risen Christ defeats despair without asking humanity to become less human.

Therefore every consequential AI system should be judged by a question sharper than "Does it work?"

Does it serve the person, or optimize the person away?
Does it tell the truth, or manage perception?
Does it strengthen responsibility, or dissolve it?
Does it protect the vulnerable, or make them easier to exploit?
Does it preserve room for prayer, silence, repentance, grief, craft, worship, and human judgment?
Does it keep the machine a tool, or train us to treat the tool as a savior?

The danger is real. Despair is not Lord.

The opposite of doom is not hype.

The opposite of doom is Christ.

Machines can serve, but they cannot save.

Let every power bend toward the Lamb, or it will bend toward an idol.
